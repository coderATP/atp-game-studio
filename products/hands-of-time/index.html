ENTER THE TIMELINE

A card game where time is the board.

Hands of Time transforms a standard deck of playing cards into a system of temporal strategy. Your hand contains cards associated with the Past, Present and Future, while Aces and Kings become anomalies that challenge you to construct specific combinations.

Every turn presents a different objective. You may need to connect cards from different periods, create a sequence, match suits, build pairs or assemble a more demanding combination. The cards you choose to play matter, but so do the cards you keep, the resources you generate and the actions you reserve for later.

MANAGE YOUR RESOURCES

Every action has a cost or opportunity.

Hands of Time gives you more to manage than the cards themselves. Your available resources determine how much flexibility you have during a turn.

Draw Points allow you to take additional cards from the deck. Swap Points allow you to exchange a card with the deck. Resource Points accumulate the value generated by cards and completed anomalies.

STRATEGY

The best card isn't always the card you play.

Hands of Time rewards planning beyond the immediate anomaly. A card may be useful because of its value, its time period, its suit or the resources it can generate.

Future cards, for example, provide more Draw Points than Past cards, while Present cards provide both Draw and Swap Points. Holding the right card can therefore give you options later, even when it does not immediately complete the anomaly in front of you.

The decision to retain or recycle your remaining cards after an anomaly adds another layer of planning. You must consider what you have already gained, what the next turn may require and whether your current hand is worth preserving.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Before you enter the timeline.

A few quick answers to common questions about Hands of Time.

Hands of Time is a strategic single-player card game by ATP Game Studio. Players use cards from different periods of time to solve temporal anomalies and progress through increasingly difficult challenges.

Numbered cards 2–4 represent the Past, 5–7 represent the Present and 8–10 represent the Future. Their temporal category affects both anomaly objectives and the resources they provide.

Aces and Kings are removed from the normal playing deck and become Time Paradoxes and Time Rifts. Each anomaly presents a specific card combination that must be completed to progress.

Draw Points allow you to draw cards from the deck, while Swap Points allow you to exchange a card from your hand with a random card from the deck.

The resolved anomaly is moved forward in the game's progression, rewards are collected and you are given choices about how to prepare your cards for the next anomaly.

Jacks act as Time Thieves and can disrupt resources or cards. Queens act as Time Agents; depending on their suit and role, they can generate resources or provide special utility during anomaly resolution.

Hands of Time is available to play in a modern web browser. No traditional installation is required to enter the game.
