How Teachers Can Review and Grade Theory Submissions on Knovilla

Learn how to access the examiner dashboard, review students' theory answers, assign marks, provide constructive feedback and send graded results.

Theory assessments allow learners to demonstrate their understanding through written responses rather than relying exclusively on objective question formats such as multiple-choice and true-or-false questions.

Because theory questions may require explanations, calculations or other detailed responses, evaluating them often involves reviewing the student's work against the question and its associated marking information.

Knovilla provides a teacher dashboard for accounts with the examiner role. Through this interface, authorised examiners can access submitted assessments, review student responses, assign marks and communicate graded results.

This guide explains the grading workflow, from opening a submission to managing completed assessment records.

1. Sign in with an examiner account

Begin by opening Knovilla in your browser.

Sign in using an account that has the examiner role. This role provides access to the teacher-facing features required for reviewing and grading supported theory submissions.

Once authenticated, navigate to the Teacher Dashboard. This is where you can access the available assessment management actions.

If the expected examiner controls are not available, confirm that you are signed in with the appropriate account and role.

2. Browse active and archived submissions

The teacher dashboard provides access to submission records, including active and archived entries.

Browse the available submissions and identify the assessment you intend to review. Check the information displayed for each record to avoid selecting the wrong submission.

Active records are useful when working through submissions that require attention, while archived records provide a way to organise submissions that have been moved out of the active view.

Once you have identified the relevant submission, select its available grading action to open the assessment.

3. Read the questions and student responses

Before assigning marks, carefully examine the assessment questions and the student's submitted answers.

Read each response in relation to the question being asked. Pay attention to the student's reasoning, accuracy, use of relevant terminology and ability to address the required parts of the question.

Where supported images are included in a submission, review them alongside the associated written work.

Images may contain information that is important to the student's answer, so avoid grading a response based only on its written portion when additional submitted material is available.

Compare the response with the question's marking information before deciding how many marks to award.

4. Award marks for each question part

Knovilla allows the examiner to enter marks for the individual parts of a theory submission.

Review the maximum score associated with each part before entering the student's awarded mark.

The mark entered should remain within the permitted maximum. Assess the response according to the available marking information and the quality of the submitted work.

For questions containing multiple parts, evaluate each part separately rather than assigning a score based solely on your overall impression of the submission.

Consistent marking helps ensure that students are assessed fairly against the same expectations.

5. Add useful examiner comments

In addition to awarding marks, examiners can provide comments on submitted work.

Effective feedback should help the learner understand what they did well and what could be improved.

Rather than writing only a general remark, consider identifying a specific strength or explaining an important omission.

For example, a response may demonstrate an understanding of the main concept but omit an explanation required by the question. A brief comment identifying that omission can provide useful guidance.

Keep your comments professional, relevant and focused on the student's work. Constructive feedback makes the assessment more valuable as a learning activity.

6. Review and save the grading

After entering the marks and comments, review the grading information before saving it.

Check that each question part has been assessed appropriately and that no awarded mark exceeds its maximum score.

Also review your comments for clarity and ensure that the grading corresponds to the correct student submission.

Use the available save action to submit the grading information to Knovilla's backend.

Wait for the application to complete the saving process before proceeding to another action.

7. Send the graded result

Saving the grading and sending the result are separate parts of the assessment workflow.

After the grading has been saved, use the dashboard's available result-sending action to send the graded result.

Before doing so, confirm that the marks and feedback are complete and accurate.

Follow the instructions presented by the application and allow the action to finish.

This additional review helps reduce the likelihood of sending incomplete or incorrectly graded work.

8. Archive or restore submission records

The teacher dashboard also provides submission management actions for archiving and restoring records.

Archiving can help keep the active submission view organised, particularly when managing multiple assessments.

If an archived record needs to be returned to the active view, use the available restore action.

Review the selected record carefully before performing either action.

Maintaining an organised dashboard can make it easier to identify submissions that still require grading or other examiner attention.

Good Practices for Grading Theory Assessments

Effective theory grading involves more than entering numerical scores. It requires careful reading, consistent judgement and feedback that supports learning.

Review the marking information before assessing responses. Consider each question part independently and examine all supported submitted materials, including relevant images.

Avoid rushing through submissions or allowing a student's performance on one question to influence unrelated marks.

Where comments are appropriate, explain important strengths and areas for improvement clearly. Before sending results, verify the recorded marks and confirm that the correct submission has been graded.

A thoughtful grading process helps turn theory assessments into opportunities for students to understand their performance and improve their subject knowledge.
